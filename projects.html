  1. Bayesian Evidence on the Random Walk in Intraday Price Paths

    Oct 2026·Independent·first version Jul 2024

    How much evidence do 5-hour price paths in E-mini S&P 500 futures (ES), EUR/USD and Bitcoin (BTC) give for or against the random walk from October 2024 to September 2026, and how sensitive is it to the prior?

    For EUR/USD and BTC, the Bayes factors in favor of the random walk rise from about 1 to between 4 and 12 as the prior widens, so the strength of the evidence depends largely on the prior. The ES paths rise more often than they fall, but reordering the returns within each path leaves this almost unchanged, so it reflects the drift and distribution of hourly returns, not dependence between them. Under the unit-information prior, none of 9 lagged predictors is selected or improves forecasts materially.

  2. Do Cointegration-based Pairs Remain Profitable?

    Oct 2026·Independent·first version Jun 2024

    Does cointegration-based pair formation in the S&P 500 remain profitable out of sample from 2010 to 2025, and does it depend on the formation method, the stock grouping or the window length?

    No formation choice is consistently profitable: in the 2020–2025 testing period, the top-decile spreads of every method, those with the strongest formation evidence against a random-walk null, lose 0.70% to 1.34% a year after trading and borrowing costs, and their mean reversion persists little longer than that of spreads formed on random walks.

  3. Price Path Embeddings in a Hang Seng Index Portfolio

    Aug 2026·Independent·first version Nov 2024

    Do PCA and VAE embeddings of a stock's past-year returns and technical indicators predict the next-month returns of Hang Seng Index stocks from September 2021 to August 2026, and what do they deliver in a long-only portfolio with a derivatives overlay?

    The forecast has a rank information coefficient near 0.07 (t near 2.5) and amounts to past-year momentum; the VAE does no better than PCA. The PCA portfolio with delta-hedged short calls and a put-spread collar returns 6.28% a year against 3.09% for the HSI total return (information ratio 0.46), but neither the active return nor the gain from the derivatives is significant.

  4. Did Liquidity Govern Stablecoin Peg Pass-Through? Evidence from the March 2023 USDC Depeg

    Jul 2026·Independent·IAQF 2026·first version Feb 2026

    Did liquidity govern how peg stress passed through to Bitcoin's price during the March 2023 USDC depeg, with the cross-currency basis on 1-minute Binance.US data split into peg and triangular arbitrage deviations?

    Liquidity did not measurably govern the pass-through: the USDC/USD illiquidity effect was significant at 1-minute sampling but weakened at 5-minute sampling. The peg deviation carried almost the entire variance of the basis, and the arbitrage deviation closed with a half-life below 1 minute, against about 1 hour for the USDC peg and over 2.5 hours for USDT, so the stress that reached Bitcoin's price was the peg itself, not a failure of arbitrage.

  5. Multi-Asset Quanto Pricing via C++ and Python Interface

    Apr 2026·Group course project·QFGB 8960, Fordham, with Alex Wong

    How do the asset–FX and asset–asset correlations move the prices of quanto options, basket calls and worst-of digitals, computed in closed form, by Monte Carlo and by finite-difference PDE in a C++ library with a Python interface?

    The three methods agree within their numerical error and satisfy put–call parity. The asset–FX correlation enters only through the drift: a higher correlation raises calls and lowers puts. A quanto basket call is cheaper than the weighted average of its component calls, and both basket calls and worst-of digital calls become more expensive as the assets become more correlated.

  6. Covariance Estimators and Out-of-Sample Hedging of SOFR Swap Risk

    Mar 2026·Course project·QFGB 8934, Fordham

    Does the choice of covariance estimator (equal-weighted, Ledoit–Wolf and OAS shrinkage, or exponential weighting) change out-of-sample hedge performance and risk forecasts of a 14-tenor SOFR swap portfolio in 2025?

    No estimator improves hedging consistently: shrinkage leaves principal-component hedges unchanged and worsens up to 38 of 79 two-swap minimum-variance hedges, while exponential weighting improves 36 or 37 of the 79 two-swap principal-component hedges. The estimator matters for the risk forecast: exponential weighting with a decay factor of 0.94 shows no significant bias, equal weighting overstates unhedged risk as volatility falls, and shrinkage overstates hedged residual risk by a factor of 2.5 to 4.

  8. Swarm Velocity Dynamics in US Sector ETFs

    Feb 2026·Course project·QFGB 890P, Fordham

    Does the leader-following swarm velocity of Chen, Huang and Tang (2025), the speed at which each asset closes its gap to the leader, carry tradable information in the eleven Select Sector SPDR ETFs?

    The velocity filters out unprofitable trades but offers no standalone tradable edge: the fast-minus-slow velocity sort returns 18.1% from October 2018 to December 2025 (Sharpe ratio 0.33), against 94.5% (0.65) for an equally weighted portfolio. Because the sectors are highly correlated, the estimated speed is negative, the reverse of the positive speeds Chen, Huang and Tang report.

  9. Seasonality Anomalies in the US Equity Market

    Jun 2025·Independent·first version Oct 2023

    How strong is monthly and daily calendar seasonality in the S&P 500 from 1981 to 2023, and do the Halloween and turn-of-month strategies beat buy and hold?

    November to April exceeds May to October by 0.81% a month, driven mainly by a weak September and a strong year-end. The combined Halloween and turn-of-month strategy matches buy and hold with dividends in return, volatility and Sharpe ratio (0.377 against 0.376), with a maximum drawdown 7% shallower. Its rolling outperformance declines over time and is not significant.

  10. Do Trump's Tweets Spur Market Volatility?

    Aug 2024·Independent

    Are Donald Trump's tweets, scored for sentiment by VADER and assigned topics by BERTopic, associated with daily changes in the VIX from January 2016 to January 2021?

    In the session that first reflects a tweet of extreme sentiment, the log change in the VIX is 0.95% higher (about 0.14 index points, significant at 5%), and the difference does not extend to later sessions. The coronavirus tweets came after the VIX had risen and the election-fraud tweets as it fell, consistent with tweets that comment on news rather than create it.

  11. Does the Distance Approach to Pairs Trading Survive in the Modern Era?

    Jun 2024·Independent

    Does the distance approach to pairs trading of Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst (2006) remain profitable for US large caps from June 2014 to May 2024, net of costs?

    None of the six portfolios (three distances, each ranked by distance alone or by a composite score) earns a significant return, with mean returns between −0.79% and 0.44% a year, and outside the COVID half-year (December 2019 to May 2020) all six lose money. Only 36% to 41% of trades converge within their six-month window.

  12. A Comparison of Neural Architectures for Movie Poster Genre Classification

    May 2024·Group course project·STAT 4012, CUHK, six members

    How well do convolutional, transfer-learned and vision-language models classify a film's genre from its poster?

    ResNets deliver the strongest results, 54.5% accuracy on 19 genres and 69.7% on 3: a poster carries enough genre signal to be worth modeling but not enough for a reliable label. Adding the release decade as an input raises the accuracy by about 4%.

  14. Principal Components of US Sector Prices and Macroeconomic Data

    Dec 2023·Group course project·RMSC 4002, CUHK, six members

    What do the principal components of ten US macroeconomic indicators and eleven sector ETFs describe, and how well do the sector components explain the daily S&P 500 return?

    Two components summarize each dataset: inflation moving with consumption and a wage–production contrast explain 78.26% of the macroeconomic variance, and a market-wide and an energy factor 93.62% of the sector price variance. A regression on the first two sector-return components explains 97.14% of the variance of the S&P 500 return in the training sample and has a lower test-sample error than one component (RMSE 0.206% against 0.266%).

  15. Calibrating the Coupon Strike of an Autocallable Note under Local Volatility

    Which coupon strike gives a two-year worst-of autocallable note on the Nikkei 225, the S&P 500 and the Hang Seng Index a price of 98% of issue, by Monte Carlo under a Dupire local volatility surface?

    At the contractual coupon rates of 2% and 0.01%, no strike does: with coupons well below the two-year USD rate of 4.83%, the note is worth less than 98% of issue at any strike. With the two rates free, the required coupon strike rises with both.
