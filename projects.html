  1. Efficiency Loss of Sequential Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimation in the AR(p)-EGARCH(1,1) Model

    Oct 2026·Independent

    This study examines which parameters of the AR(p)-EGARCH(1,1) model are estimated less efficiently by sequential than by joint Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood estimation. Even without a leverage effect, the sequential estimator loses efficiency for the coefficient on the lagged innovation when the mean return is estimated and the innovation kurtosis is below an explicit threshold. With leverage, the loss extends to the intercept and persistence of the log variance and is largest for the mean log variance, whose asymptotic variance rises by about 15% in the base simulation design and by 17% to 21% at the S&P 500 estimate. A three-step estimator that refits the mean by feasible generalized least squares recovers most of the efficiency for the mean parameters.

  2. An Introduction to AR-EGARCH Models and Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimation

    Sep 2026·Independent

    This note introduces AR-EGARCH models and Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood estimation, from time series foundations and the stylized facts of asset returns to volatility filtering and forecast evaluation. It explains why sequential estimation of the variance parameters is as efficient as joint estimation when the information matrix is block diagonal, as in ARMA models with GARCH errors and symmetric innovations. Even without a leverage effect, EGARCH lacks the symmetry that guarantees this block diagonality, which motivates the accompanying efficiency study.

  3. Bayesian Evidence on the Random Walk in Intraday Price Paths

    Oct 2026·Independent·first version Jul 2024

    This study examines 5-hour price trajectories of E-mini S&P 500 futures, EUR/USD and Bitcoin from 2024 to 2026 using Bayes factors to test the random walk hypothesis. For EUR/USD and Bitcoin, Bayes factors favor the random walk, though the strength of this evidence depends largely on the prior. The E-mini S&P 500 futures show only weak evidence against the random walk.

  4. Covariance Estimators and Out-of-Sample Hedging of SOFR Swap Risk

    Mar 2026·Course project·QFGB 8934, Fordham

    For a SOFR swap portfolio in 2025, this project asks whether the covariance estimator changes out-of-sample hedging and risk forecasting. A DV01 profile over 14 tenors is hedged with one or two swaps under five covariance estimators: the equal-weighted sample covariance, Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage, oracle approximating shrinkage and exponential weighting at two decay factors. No estimator improves hedging consistently in Diebold–Mariano tests with Newey–West standard errors, but the choice matters for the risk forecast. Shrinkage overstates the variance of hedged portfolios by a factor of 2.5 to 4, because shrinking toward a scaled identity inflates the small variance left after hedging.

  5. Did Liquidity Govern Stablecoin Peg Pass-Through? Evidence from the March 2023 USDC Depeg

    Jul 2026·Independent·first version Feb 2026

    This study decomposes each stablecoin's cross-currency basis into a peg deviation and a triangular arbitrage deviation using 1-minute Binance.US prices of Bitcoin, USDT and USDC. A pass-through regression then tests whether lagged illiquidity changes how the arbitrage deviation responds to the peg deviation. Results show that liquidity did not measurably govern the pass-through during the March 2023 USDC depeg. Instead, the stress that reached Bitcoin's stablecoin price was the peg deviation itself rather than a failure of arbitrage. (A revision of an IAQF 2026 Student Competition entry.)

  6. Do Cointegration-based Pairs Remain Profitable?

    Oct 2026·Independent·first version Jun 2024

    This study forms S&P 500 spreads within sectors or correlation-based clusters using pairs, Johansen cointegration, principal component analysis, eigenanalysis and stock-versus-group cointegration over 1- to 5-year windows. Out-of-sample results show that none of these formation methods are independently profitable. From 2020 to 2025, the top-decile spreads of all methods incur annual losses of 0.70% to 1.34% after costs.

  7. Price Path Embeddings in a Hang Seng Index Portfolio

    Aug 2026·Independent·first version Nov 2024

    This project utilizes PCA and LSTM variational autoencoder (VAE) embeddings of each stock's past year to forecast returns for a long-only HSI portfolio. Listed HSI derivatives are incorporated through delta-hedged short calls and a conditional put-spread collar. While the return forecast is statistically significant, it primarily captures past-year momentum. The VAE performs similarly to PCA, and the portfolio's advantage over the HSI total return is not statistically significant.

  8. Multi-Asset Quanto Pricing via C++ and Python Interface

    Apr 2026·Group course project·QFGB 8960, Fordham, with Alex Wong

    We extend qflib, a C++ library with a Python interface, to price European quanto options in closed form, by Monte Carlo simulation and by a finite-difference PDE scheme. Since the asset–FX correlation enters through the drift, a higher correlation raises call prices and lowers put prices. Quanto basket calls and worst-of digital calls, priced by Monte Carlo, both become more expensive as the assets become more correlated.

  9. Knowledge Graphs of Bank Volatility in the COVID-19 Crisis

    Mar 2026·Course project·QFGB 890P, Fordham

    Building on the knowledge graph approach of Chen and Zhang (2024), this study investigates whether the volatilities of 70 US-listed banks were more interconnected during the COVID-19 crisis than in non-crisis periods. Edges among the 25 largest of them are established when the graphical lasso partial correlation surpasses a uniform threshold. The crisis network is denser than the non-crisis network only at low partial correlations. Above a cutoff of 0.040, the non-crisis network becomes denser.

  10. Seasonality Anomalies in the US Equity Market

    Jun 2025·Independent·first version Oct 2023

    Using S&P 500 data from 1981 to 2023, this project examines what remains of monthly and daily calendar seasonality and whether the Halloween and turn-of-month effects still pay as trading strategies. November to April outperforms May to October by a statistically significant 0.81% a month, driven by a weak September and a strong year end. A combined Halloween and turn-of-month strategy slightly outperforms buy and hold, although the lead is not statistically significant.

  11. Do Trump's Tweets Spur Market Volatility?

    Aug 2024·Independent

    From January 2016 to January 2021, this study measures the daily association between Donald Trump's tweets and the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX). Each tweet is assigned a topic with BERTopic and scored for sentiment with VADER, and regressions relate the daily log change in the VIX around each tweet session to an indicator of extreme sentiment and to the number of tweets on each topic. Only a few of the 19 topics have significant coefficients. Tweets on election fraud carry negative ones, as the VIX fell after the November 2020 election, while neither the coronavirus nor the China trade topic is significant at the 5% level at any horizon. Most coronavirus tweets came after the VIX had peaked in March 2020, consistent with tweets that comment on news rather than create it.

  12. Does the Distance Approach to Pairs Trading Survive in the Modern Era?

    Jun 2024·Independent

    Using Euclidean, Manhattan and Chebyshev distances, this project applies the Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst (2006) distance approach to pairs trading in US large caps from June 2014 to May 2024. Pairs are formed within sectors and ranked by distance or by a composite score that adds zero crossings and spread volatility to the distance, then traded over 20 half-years after costs. No portfolio earns a statistically significant return, and outside the COVID half-year from December 2019 to May 2020, every portfolio loses money.

  13. Swarm Velocity Dynamics in US Sector ETFs

    Feb 2026·Course project·QFGB 890P, Fordham

    At daily frequency, this study tests whether the leader-following swarm velocity of Chen, Huang and Tang (2025) carries tradable information for 11 Select Sector SPDR ETFs. A threshold rule applies the velocity to a leader-versus-loser pair trade, and a cross-sectional rule ranks the ETFs by their own speeds. The estimated speeds lie close to the value that returns without leader-following produce, because the gap to the leader is dated on the same day as the velocity. The velocity threshold cuts transaction costs but does not pick better trades, and neither rule earns a significantly positive return.

  14. Calibrating the Coupon Strike of an Autocallable Note under Local Volatility

    Dec 2023·Group course project·FINA 4150, CUHK, three members

    We price a 2-year worst-of autocallable note on the Nikkei 225, the S&P 500 and the Hang Seng Index by Monte Carlo simulation under a Dupire local volatility surface calibrated to Bloomberg option chains. The note pays a 2% coupon if the worst-performing index meets or exceeds the coupon strike, and only 0.01% otherwise. At these contractual terms, no coupon strike allows the note to price at 98% of its issue price, as the 2% coupon is far below the prevailing 2-year USD discount rate of 4.83%.

  15. Confidence-Weighted Pairs Trading via DCC-EGARCH

    Apr 2024·Group course project·FINA 4380, CUHK, four members

    In a backtest of US equities and ETFs from August 2021 to December 2023, we evaluate whether individualized entry and exit thresholds and confidence weights enhance a pairs trading portfolio. Pairs are identified using the Engle–Granger cointegration test, with hedge ratios estimated via a Kalman filter and spread volatility modeled with a DCC-EGARCH approach. Net of transaction costs, optimized thresholds increase total returns but reduce the Sharpe ratio from 1.32 to 1.17. Confidence weights, calculated from rolling cointegration p-values and forecast volatility, yield the highest Sharpe ratio of 1.43.

  16. Principal Components of US Sector Prices and Macroeconomic Data

    Dec 2023·Group course project·RMSC 4002, CUHK, six members

    We apply principal component analysis to determine how many components effectively summarize 10 monthly US macroeconomic indicators and the prices of the 11 Select Sector SPDR ETFs. For each dataset, two principal components suffice: the first macroeconomic component captures inflation moving with consumption and the second captures wage growth relative to production, while the sector components reflect a broad market factor and an energy-specific factor. We then regress daily S&P 500 log returns on the same-day sector return components from June 2018 to November 2023. The two-component model explains 97.14% of in-sample return variance and outperforms a single-component approach in the testing period.

  17. A Comparison of Neural Architectures for Movie Poster Genre Classification

    May 2024·Group course project·STAT 4012, CUHK, six members

    We examine whether the genre of a film can be recovered from its poster alone, and at what computational cost. VGG16, AlexNet, ResNet-18, ResNet-50 and YOLOv8 are adapted by transfer learning, and CLIP is applied zero-shot. These and a convolutional network trained from scratch are compared with EfficientNetV2 and FastViT, two backbones built for efficient inference. ResNets attain the highest accuracy, at 54.5% on the 19-genre task and 69.7% on the 3-genre task. Supplying the decade as an indicator raises the convolutional network's accuracy by about 4%.
